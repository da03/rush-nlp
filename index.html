My research in natural language processing and machine learning explores how neural models can learn computations that would otherwise be expressed through symbolic programs or explicit reasoning steps. My group develops methods for compiling natural-language function specifications into reusable neural programs (ProgramAsWeights), simulating interactive computing environments with generative models (NeuralOS), and internalizing reasoning through implicit chain of thought. I also study how people use language models in the wild through large-scale conversation datasets and open analysis tools, including WildChat and WildVis.

I build open-source systems and interactive demos that make these ideas concrete and let others explore, evaluate, and build on them. Our WildChat dataset has been used by OpenAI and Anthropic for model safety evaluation, and I co-developed OpenNMT, an open-source neural machine translation toolkit widely used in research and industry. I received my PhD from Harvard University, advised by Alexander Rush and Stuart Shieber, and was a postdoctoral researcher with Yejin Choi at the Allen Institute for AI.

From Problem Solvers to Tool Builders

I believe pretraining models to compile repeated tasks into small, reusable neural programs - instead of always solving problems from scratch - is one of the most exciting future directions in AI.
