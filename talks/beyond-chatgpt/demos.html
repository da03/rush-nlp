Follow the evidence

  • NeuralOS paper. The 62% memory result in the talk concerns created-folder tests after 256 frames. It is one test condition, not an overall accuracy score.
  • Program-as-Weights paper. The demos combine neural programs with conventional application code.
  • AskChem paper. AI extracts structured claims from papers and preserves links to their supporting evidence.
  • Sandford et al., A synthetic chemist’s guide to electroanalytical tools for studying reaction mechanisms. Chemical Science, 2019, section 2.1. The excerpt in the slides comes from this paper, licensed CC BY-NC 3.0.
  • SPARK’s supplied RuHex measurement. The talk plots this experimental example. The two mechanism alternatives are a separate teaching example based on SPARK’s synthetic case. Changing acidity is a proposed test, not a completed experiment. See the project README for the model’s limitations.
  • WildChat. The one-million-conversation figure in the talk refers to an earlier release.

NeuralOS and PAW recordings come from the presenters’ research materials. The training screens, weight updates and SPARK curve comparisons are teaching illustrations. Live demos and source links require internet access.
